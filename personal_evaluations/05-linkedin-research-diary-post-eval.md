## 📌 Summary

**Overall progress assessment**

The *idea* is strong and genuinely rare: most people post about tool choices, very few post about the memory problem that causes the tool search. The topic is original and the two-stage narrative gives it structure. The problem is that the English error density is high enough that the post currently reads as unedited, and — more damaging — the single best insight in it is buried inside a sentence a native speaker cannot parse.

| Criterion | Verdict |
|---|---|

| **Structure** | 🟡 Adequate — two clear stages, but the best part is buried |

**Key area of improvement:** the draft confuses *talking* with *writing*. Speaking allows ungrammatical connective tissue that a reader repairs automatically. Writing does not — every error stops the reader for a moment. Public technical writing needs a stricter register than speech.

**Area needing work:** verb tense discipline, and the habit of putting one idea in one sentence.

## 📊 Assessment Details

### Session 7: LinkedIn post — research diary (2026-09-28)

**Topic covered:** a ~270-word LinkedIn post explaining the author's shift from Notion + NotebookLM to GitHub + an AI agent for a research diary.

**Overall strengths**
- The problem statement is honest and specific: *"I can't retain a whole lesson's content in one day."*
- The two-stage structure (Notion → GitHub) is easy to follow and gives the post a narrative.
- The author chose free tools and plain text, which reads as intentional rather than as poverty.
- The core insight — that you cannot write *during* a lecture, so you must externalise — is the most valuable line in the whole post, and it is entirely original to the author.

**Overall weaknesses**
- Sentence 3, 11 and 16 are not parseable without rereading.
- Tense shifts without warning: past in sentences 9–12, present in 13 and 18.
- The AI agent — the only genuinely novel technical component — gets one clause.
- No numbers anywhere: no note count, no duration, no streak.

### ✏️ Line-by-line correction log

#### Title (sentences 1–2)

> ❌ *How **should** I start a research-diary? How **can** I keep my discipline to write down it following day to day?*

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `research-diary` | **research diary** | Compound **nouns** take no hyphen |
| `should` / `can` mismatch | one auxiliary for both questions | Parallel structure in a list of questions |
| `keep my discipline to` | **stay disciplined enough to** | `discipline` = self-control here, not sport |
| `write down it` | **write it down** | `down` is a particle that follows the object |
| `following day to day` | **day after day** | Fixed idiom — see vocabulary note §6 |

#### Sentence 3 — ⚠️ unreadable

> ❌ *I can not remember all lesson's content in a day when the dark fall down at each the end of the day.*

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `can not` | **cannot** (formal) / **can't** (conversational) | Two words only in casual emphasis |
| `all lesson's content` | **everything in a lesson** / **a whole lesson's content** | `all` cannot front a possessive like this |
| `the dark fall` | **the dark falls** | Subject–verb agreement; 3rd person `-s` |
| `fall down` | **falls** | `fall down` means *to topple*; darkness does not topple |
| `at each the end` | **at the end** | `each` is redundant with the definite article |
| *(meaning)* | state the loss explicitly: by day's end it is gone | Recover the intended causal link |

💡 **Teachable point:** `the dark falls` is the idiomatic literary way to say *"trời tối dần"*. It is worth memorising as a fixed phrase, not composed word by word.

#### Sentence 4 — inverted logic

> ❌ *And I feel uncomfortable **enough** to write down each lesson during a day.*

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `uncomfortable enough to` | **I find it hard to write…** / **I'm too self-conscious to write…** | `enough` *increases* degree — the exact opposite of the intended meaning |

💡 `too … to` and `find it + adjective + to` are the correct structures for *quá … để*. The single word `enough` reversed the whole clause.

#### Sentence 5

> ❌ *But I want to keep on recording and skimming through them in my free time to **evolve my weakness** and **increase my ability** every single day.*

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **evolve my weakness** | **work on / address my weak spots** | Weaknesses are *fixed*, not evolved. A native reader stops here |
| **increase my ability** | **build / improve my skills** | `increase` takes quantities, not capabilities |
| `keep on recording` | **keep recording** | `keep on` + bare infinitive is redundant in writing |
| `them` | **those lessons** | Dangling pronoun — antecedent is unclear |

#### Sentence 6 — 🔴 serious spelling error

> ❌ *So I **though** to make it easy as good as I can for free.*

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **I though** | **I thought** | `though` = *however*; `thought` = *decided*. The most damaging error in the post |
| `easy as good as I can` | **as easy and as good as I could** | Comparative correlative: `as + adj + as` must repeat `as` |

#### Sentence 7

> ❌ *In that journey, I crossed over 2 stages from using ready-made integrated technologies to **self-connected technologies**:*

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `crossed over 2 stages` | **went through two stages** | Spell out numbers under 10 in prose |
| **self-connected technologies** | **tools I own and control** | Not an English word. Also false: GitHub is *hosted*, not self-hosted |
| *technologies* | **tools** | `technologies` is too grand for Notion/NotebookLM |

#### Sentence 8 (heading)

> ❌ *Stage#1: Notion, notebook LM's **unconvenient**:*

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `NotebookLM's` | **convenient but limiting** | Possessive cannot express an adjective meaning |
| **unconvenient** | **outgrew them** / **convenient but limiting** | Not an English word |
| — | add a verb | Headings need a predicate to be readable |

#### Sentence 9

> ❌ ***At the first time**, I **used to leverage** Notion and notebook LM for knowledge collection.*

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **At the first time** | **At first** | This structure does not exist in English |
| **used to leverage** | **I started with** / **I used** | `used to` describes a *past habit*, not a single completed action |
| `NotebookLM` | **NotebookLM** | One word, capital LM |
| `for knowledge collection` | **to collect knowledge** | Avoid nominalisations; `to + verb` is more direct |

#### Sentence 10

> ❌ *That was quite good because of **free saving**, and **Using** AI to interact with those notes.*

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **free saving** | **free storage** | `saving` = *economy*; `storage` = *storing data* |
| **Using** (capitalised) | **using** | Capital only at a sentence start |
| run-on structure | **It worked well: free storage, and I could interact with those notes through AI.** | One idea per clause |

#### Sentence 11 — ⚠️ unreadable

> ❌ *But it is limited with **AI's interacted threshold ability** lead to be **unchangeable** for flexible management…*

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `AI's interacted threshold ability` | **the context threshold** | The author already uses `context` correctly in sentence 12 — inconsistency |
| `lead to be` | — | Dangling infinitive; the whole clause needs restructuring |
| **unchangeable** | **rigid** / **hard to reorganise** | Not idiomatic; `rigid` is the natural word |

**Recast:** *But it had limits: once the context threshold was reached, the notes became rigid and hard to reorganise.*

#### Sentence 12 — ⚠️ recurring error

> ❌ *…and it **trend** to out context if I research **to much**.*

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `trend` | **trends** | Subject–verb agreement |
| `out context` | **run out of context** | Requires the verb particle `run` and the preposition `of` |
| **to much** | **too much** | 🔴 **RECURRING** — identical error in IELTS Session 3 (`no1_...:57`) |

#### Sentences 13, 15, 18 — tense and agreement

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `So I change to manage…` | **So I changed my approach.** | Past tense; the whole narrative is retrospective |
| `these things **is** written` | **these notes are written** | Subject–verb agreement |
| `written down into` | **written into** | Redundant particle |
| `markdown file` | **Markdown files** | Markdown is always capitalised; plural required |
| `with logical format` | **with a consistent structure** | `format` needs an article; `consistent structure` is idiomatic |
| `push up` (bare infinitive in a passive chain) | **pushed** | Participle must match `are written` |
| **configurated** | **configured** | 🔴 Wrong word form entirely |
| `by my configured AI agent` | **with the help of an AI agent I configured** | `by` implies passive authorship; also `configured` misdirects |
| `And still maintain that everyday` | **And I can still maintain it every day** | Missing subject; `everyday` → `every day` (two words, adverb) |
| `exchangeable structure` | **modular structure** / **portable structure** | `exchangeable` means *interchangeable goods*; Markdown's property is portability |

#### Sentence 16 — ⚠️ unreadable

> ❌ *So with that solution, **It get already solve** all my lessons, then be expanded critically to help me **understand deeper** any problem.*

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **It get already solve** | **everything from a lecture gets captured** | The subject must be *everything*, not *it* |
| `then be expanded` | **then expanded critically** | `be` is wrong in a non-passive clause chain |
| **understand deeper** | **understand more deeply** | 🔴 `deeper` is a comparative **adjective**; an adverb needs `-ly` |
| `understand deeper any problem` | **understand any problem more deeply** | Adverb placement |

#### Sentence 17

> ❌ *Overall, I **successfully** created a method **in** deep knowledge management.*

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `successfully` | delete | Filler adverb; the claim is implied by the evidence |
| `method in` | **method for** | `for` = purpose; `in` = location or domain |
| `deep knowledge management` | **in-depth knowledge management** / **second-brain knowledge management** | The hyphenated compound is the idiomatic form |

### 🎯 Top three fixes, by impact

1. **`I though` → `I thought`** — a spelling error in the opening third of the post is the fastest way to lose a technical reader.
2. **`to much` → `too much`** — second occurrence. This belongs on a personal error list, not just in a correction table.
3. **One idea per sentence.** Sentences 11, 12 and 16 each carry two or three ideas joined by commas. On a phone screen, which is where LinkedIn is mostly read, the reader gives up.

**Secondary but structural:** tense. The post narrates a completed journey but slips into present tense three times (`it trend`, `I change`, `still maintain`). Use **past** for the journey, **present** only for the current state of the system.

---

### 💎 Content strategy — the insight is in the wrong place

The post is built around **tool comparison**, but the valuable claim is the **memory problem**. Tool posts are forgettable; problem posts are shared. Restructure so the insight leads and the tools support it.

**Three academic phrases that upgrade the post instantly**

| Term | Meaning | Sentence it unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| **cognitive offloading** | moving a memory task out of the head onto an external system | *"A research diary is **cognitive offloading** on paper."* 🔥 |
| **externalise** | to move something outside oneself (vs *internalise*) | *"I **externalise** what I hear in a lecture."* |
| **second brain** | an external system you actually maintain | *"I built a **second brain** I still maintain."* |

**Source for the academic framing:** Risko, E. F., & Gilbert, S. J. (2016). "Cognitive Offloading." *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 20(9), 676–688. The paper formalises exactly the mechanism the author is describing — using physical action to enhance cognitive performance.

**Theoretical lineage the author can legitimately claim**

| Source | Connection to this system |
|---|---|
| **Tiago Forte — *Building a Second Brain* (2022)** | **PARA** (Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives) + **progressive summarization** (summarise once, then again, then again) — the named version of the "skim every day" habit |
| **Zettelkasten (Niklas Luhmann, via Sönke Ahrens 2017)** | one idea per note, linked with double-bracket wiki links — the note-as-network model used throughout this repository |
| **Ebbinghaus forgetting curve / spaced repetition** | the reason review is daily, not one-off |

**Four structural problems and their fixes**

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| **No numbers** | *"412 notes, 6 months, zero missed days."* Specificity is what earns engagement on LinkedIn |
| **The reason for abandoning Notion is weak** | The stated reason (AI context limit) no longer holds, since Notion also has a context window. The real and stronger reason is **format ownership and portability**. Claiming the honest reason is more credible than claiming the fashionable one |
| **The AI agent is the differentiator, and it is one clause** | Give it its own paragraph: format → generate questions from weak sections → expand skimmed material → commit. This is what makes the post worth reading |
| **The ending closes nothing** | End with either a question to the reader or a single memorable claim |

### ✍️ Corrected version (author's voice preserved)

> **How do I start a research diary — and how do I stay disciplined enough to write it down day after day?**
>
> I can't retain a whole lesson's content in one day, and by the time **the dark falls** at the end of the day, it's gone. I also find it too uncomfortable to write during the lesson itself. But I want to keep recording everything, and skim through it in my free time, so I can **work on my weak spots and build my skills** a little every single day.
>
> So I decided to build something as easy — and as good — as I could, for free. Along the way I went through two stages: from ready-made, integrated tools to tools I own and control.
>
> **Stage 1 — Notion and NotebookLM: convenient, but limiting**
>
> At first I used Notion and NotebookLM to collect knowledge. It worked well: free storage, and I could interact with those notes through AI. But it had limits. Once the context threshold was reached, the notes became rigid and hard to reorganise, and the context **runs out** if I research too much. So I changed my approach.
>
> **Stage 2 — GitHub and an AI agent**
>
> In this stage I leveraged GitHub as a free, unlimited database for everything I know. Unstructured notes are written into **Markdown files with a consistent structure**, then committed and pushed to GitHub with the help of an AI agent I configured. With that setup, everything from a lecture is captured, then expanded critically, so I can understand any problem more deeply.
>
> Overall, I built a method for **in-depth** knowledge management — and I can still maintain it every day, because the structure is modular and scales.
>
> *In one sentence: a research diary is **cognitive offloading**. The tool matters less than the habit.*

### 🚀 LinkedIn-optimised version (short paragraphs, hook, numbers)

> **I built a second brain — because I can't remember a single lecture.**
>
> Here's the honest problem: I can't retain a whole lesson's content in one day. And writing during a lecture feels awkward. So I tried to keep it all in my head. It didn't work.
>
> So I built an external system instead. Over **[X] months** it now holds **[N] notes** in plain Markdown, with a consistent structure — and I have pushed it every single day.
>
> **Stage 1: Notion + NotebookLM.** Free, and I could chat with my notes. But once the context filled up, the notes turned rigid and hard to reorganise — and the format was not mine to take anywhere.
>
> **Stage 2: GitHub + an AI agent.** Everything becomes plain Markdown files. The agent formats them, generates questions from my weak sections, expands what I skimmed, and commits the result. Nothing is locked in.
>
> **The lesson I did not expect:** the tool was the easy part. The hard part was showing up daily. Version control taught me that — commit every day, review the diff later.
>
> **Cognitive offloading.** That is all a research diary is.
>
> *Curious how others handle this — do you take notes live, or afterwards?*

**Why this version performs better:** sentence 1 is a **hook with a relatable failure**; the numbers are placeholders the author can fill; sentences are one line each, which suits mobile; the AI agent is elevated to the differentiator it actually is; and the close opens a question instead of trailing off.

## 🎯 Action Items

### Recurring personal error list — memorise and re-check
- [ ] **`too much` / `too many`** — never `to much` / `to many` *(2nd occurrence: this post, 2026-09-28)*
- [ ] **`thought` vs `though`** — past tense needs `-t`; check every past-tense verb
- [ ] **`-ed` word forms** — `configured`, not `configurated`; `staged`, not `staged-up`
- [ ] **Comparative adjective vs adverb** — `understand more deeply`, not `deeper`
- [ ] **Prepositions** — `write it down`, not `write down it`; `a method for`, not `in`
- [ ] **`every day` (adverb) vs `everyday` (adjective)** — two words when it is an adverb
