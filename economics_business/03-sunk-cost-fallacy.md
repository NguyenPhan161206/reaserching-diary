## 📌 Key Takeaways (TL;DR)
- A sunk cost is money or time that has already been spent and cannot be recovered.
- The fallacy occurs when people or businesses continue investing in a failing project simply because they have already invested heavily in it, rather than evaluating its future potential.

## 🧠 Detailed Analysis

### 1. Theoretical Framework
- **Core theory:** Loss Aversion (Behavioral Economics).
- **Key assumptions:** Traditional economics assumes humans are rational and ignore sunk costs. Behavioral economics proves humans are irrational and emotionally attached to past investments.
- **How it differs:** It contradicts the classic "Homo Economicus" model, showing that emotional pain of admitting failure outweighs logical future projections.

### 2. Market Evidence & Data
- **Historical performance:** Studies in venture capital show that funds often hold onto losing startups too long (throwing good money after bad) to avoid writing off the initial investment.

### 3. Real-World Case Studies
#### Case 1: The Concorde Jet (The "Concorde Fallacy")
- **Situation:** The British and French governments continued funding the supersonic Concorde jet long after it was clear it would never be commercially viable.
- **Result:** They lost billions simply because they didn't want the money already spent to be "in vain."

#### Case 2: Software Re-writes
- **Situation:** A tech company spends $2M building a legacy monolith. A modern microservices architecture is needed.
- **Action:** Leadership refuses to rewrite the code because "we already spent $2M on it," choosing instead to patch the broken system endlessly.
- **Result:** Maintenance costs eventually exceed the cost of a full rewrite.

### 4. Business Model Implications
- **Competitive advantage:** Cultivating a company culture that celebrates "failing fast" and writing off sunk costs allows for rapid pivoting.
- **Cost structure:** Budgeting must focus on marginal costs (future costs) rather than sunk costs.

### 5. Actionable Strategies
- **Strategy 1:** Establish clear "kill criteria" for projects *before* they start. If the project hits the metric, kill it unemotionally.
- **Strategy 2:** In personal life, if a movie is boring after 30 minutes, leave the theater. The ticket money is gone whether you stay or leave. Don't lose your time too.

## 📝 Research Journey
- **Why:** I noticed I often finish bad books just because I bought them.
- **Struggle:** Overcoming the guilt of "wasting" money.
- **Aha moment:** The realization that the past cannot be changed. Only future costs and future benefits matter in decision making.
- **Career link:** In AI, if a model architecture is failing after 2 weeks of training, stop the training. Don't waste expensive GPU hours just because you already invested time setting it up.
