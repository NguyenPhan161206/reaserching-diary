## 🧠 Detailed Vocabulary Analysis

### 1. The Core Problem with *đề cập*

Vietnamese **đề cập** is one word carrying four distinct English jobs. English forces you to choose the *function* first:

| Vietnamese | Correct English | Why the difference matters |
|---|---|---|
| **đề cập** (neutral) | *mention* | Base verb — "to say something briefly" |
| **đề cập đến** | *refer to* | "to point to / to mean" — also a **deictic** verb |
| **đề cập về** (sơ qua) | *touch on / touch upon* | "to treat briefly" |
| **đề cập trong văn bản chính thức** | *state* | Formal written register (contracts, manuals) |
| **đề cập nguồn** | *cite* | Academic evidence chain |
| **đề cập vấn đề để bàn** | *raise* | "to introduce into the discussion" |
| **đề cập ám chỉ** | *allude to* | Indirect reference, no naming |
| **đề cập để nhấn mạnh** | *highlight / flag / point out* | Purpose-driven: the point is **attention** |
| **có nhắc tới** | *acknowledge* | Concede that something exists |
| **trích nguyên văn** | *quote* | Word-for-word reproduction |

### 2. Word-by-word analysis

#### 2.1 Mention
- **IPA:** `/ˈmen.ʃən/` (v) · **Word family:** mention → mentioned, mentioning · noun: *mention*, *above/below the mention of* (legal)
- **Definition & register:** The safest, most neutral verb. Can be serious or throwaway depending on the adverb: *state the problem clearly* vs *mention it in passing*.
- **Collocations:**
  - *mention that + clause* — **mandatory** pattern, note the "that"
  - *not to mention …* — "chưa nói đến", an escalator phrase for adding complications
  - *as mentioned above / as mentioned earlier / as previously mentioned*
  - *It is worth mentioning that …* — academic hedge, very high value for Writing Task 2
  - *to mention briefly / in passing / in the report*
  - *more than I mentioned earlier* — apology / clarification
- **⚠️ Trap:** *mention* is **not transitive with `about`**: ❌ *"mention about the problem"* → ✅ *"**mention the problem**."*

#### 2.2 Refer to
- **IPA:** `/rɪˈfɜː.tə/` (v) · **Word family:** refer → referred (double `r`!) → referring · noun: **reference**, **reference point**
- **Three distinct meanings (a real trap):**
  1. **Deictic / textual:** "to point back to something already said" — *I was referring to the March report.*
  2. **Semantic (most important in tech writing):** "to mean" — *In this paper, **scaling** refers to throughput per unit cost.*
  3. **Physical:** "to point to / to direct attention to" — *The diagram refers to the node in red.*
- **Collocations:** *refer to … as …* (define a term) · *referred to in the literature as* · *the term refers to* · *please refer to Appendix B* · *cross-refer to §3.2* · *refer to the table below*
- **⚠️ Trap:** **spell checkers love "refered"** — the correct past tense is **referred** (single `r` after the double).
- **⚠️ Register trap:** *refer to* in AmE is more formal; everyday speech prefers *mean* ("What does that refer to?" → "What do you mean?").

#### 2.3 State
- **IPA:** `/steɪt/` (v) · **Word family:** state → stated → noun **state** (a condition, a country) — **same word, two unrelated meanings**
- **Definition:** To express a fact, condition or requirement in a clear, fixed, official form. The backbone of contracts, specs and manuals.
- **Collocations:** *the manual states that …* · *as stated in clause 4* · *state the assumption* · *state the problem clearly* · *for the reasons stated above* · *state-of-the-art* (⚠️ different meaning!) · *in the stated time frame*
- **Why engineers should prefer it:** *state* implies the information is **authoritative and binding**; *mention* implies it was incidental. In a spec, that difference has contractual consequences.
- **⚠️ Trap:** *state* has the noun meanings "condition" and "country" — *"the model is in a good state"* / *"The United States"*. Also, *state-of-the-art* means **tiên tiến nhất**, not "trạng thái của nghệ thuật" — a common Vietnamese mistranslation.

#### 2.4 Cite
- **IPA:** `/saɪt/` (v) · **Word family:** cite → cited → citing · nouns: **citation**, **cite** (informal) · related: **quote**, **paraphrase**, **reference**
- **Definition:** To name a source, example or authority **in order to support a claim**. Academic and legal backbone word.
- **Collocations:** *cite a source / study / paper / page number* · *as cited in* · *support a claim with citations* · *citation style (APA, IEEE, MLA)* · *self-citation* · *in-text citation* · *op. cit.*
- **Research value:** In technical writing, uncited claims are invisible. *Citing* is also an **honesty mechanism** — never claim an improvement without naming the baseline you beat.
- **⚠️ Trap:** *cite* ≠ *quote* — *cite* may reference without reproducing; *quote* reproduces exact words. Also ❌ *"a cite site"* (should be *website* — a very common learner error).

#### 2.5 Raise
- **IPA:** `/reɪz/` (v) · **Word family:** raise → raised → raising · **noun** **raise** (US: a salary increase; a fundraising round — BE = *rise*)
- **Definition:** To bring a topic, issue or objection **into the discussion**.
- **Collocations:** *raise an issue / a concern / an objection / a question* · *raise a point* · *raise doubts about* · *raise awareness of* · *raise funds / capital* · *raise an alarm* · *raise the bar*
- **⚠️ Trap:** *raise* is **transitive** — ❌ *"I want to raise about the deadline"* → ✅ *"I want to **raise the deadline issue**."*

#### 2.6 Touch on / bring up / allude to (the register ladder)

| Register | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Most formal / literary | **allude to** | *She alluded to the merger without naming it.* |
| Formal / academic | **touch on** | *The paper touches on ethics but does not develop it.* |
| Neutral | **raise** / **mention** | *He raised a valid point in the meeting.* |
| Informal | **bring up** | *Don't bring up that topic again.* |

- **⚠️ `bring up` double meaning:** "raise a child" (*She brought up two children.*) — this is why formal writing prefers *raise* an issue.
- **⚠️ `touch on` ≠ `touch`:** *touch on* = deal briefly; *touch* = make physical contact. Never write *"the paper touches the ethics topic"* when you mean *touches on*.

#### 2.7 Precisely

- **IPA:** `/prɪˈsaɪs.li/` · **Word family:** **precise** (adj) · **precisely** (adv) · **precision** (n) · **imprecise** (adj) · **imprecision** (n)
- ⚠️ **Never omit the `-li`**: ❌ */prɪˈsaɪs/*

**Four meanings, four registers**

| Sense | Function | Example | Vietnamese |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Accuracy of measurement | *Tell me **precisely** how many rows were affected.* | chính xác, tỉ mỉ |
| 2 | Emphasis: "exactly this" | *That's **precisely** what I was worried about.* | **chính xác là** điều tôi lo |
| 3 | Emphatic agreement (formal) | *"You missed the deadline." — "**Precisely**."* | chính xác, đúng rồi |
| 4 | Cutting correction (**rude**) | *"I said Monday." — "**Precisely**."* | đúng chính xác đấy *(châm chọc)* |

⚠️ **Sense 4 is socially aggressive in real English.** Using *precisely* to correct someone's mistake is a face-threat. Native speakers say *"Not quite"* or *"Not exactly"* instead.

**High-value fixed phrases**

| Phrase | Meaning | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **`precisely because …`** | chính vì, chính là do đó | ⭐⭐⭐ Writing Task 2 + email |
| **`to be precise`** | nói chính xác thì | ⭐⭐⭐ correcting yourself mid-sentence |
| **`more precisely`** | nói chính xác hơn, cụ thể hơn | ⭐⭐⭐ refining a claim |
| `precisely defined / stated / measured / known` | được định nghĩa/đo chính xác | ⭐⭐⭐ academic |
| `not precisely …` | không hẳn là…, không hoàn toàn | ⭐⭐ |
| `at this precise moment` (*precise*, not *precisely*) | đúng khoảnh khắc này | ⭐⭐ |
| `precise and to the point` | chính xác và đúng trọng tâm | ⭐⭐ review writing |

### 4. Precise vs Accurate vs Exact vs Definite — the precision trap

| Word | Focus | Diagnostic example |
|---|---|---|
| **precise** | **Finesse, no vagueness** | A measurement can be *precise* (stable) and still *wrong* |
| **accurate** | **Correctness vs reality** | *The estimate is consistent but **inaccurate**.* |
| **exact** | **No error at all** (math) | *the **exact** value of π* |
| **definite** | **Certainty, no ambiguity** | *a **definite** answer* |
| **correct** | **Right conclusion** | *a **correct** conclusion* |

> 📌 **Memory anchor:** a prediction can be **precise but not accurate** — stable variance, wrong bias. This is the whole statistical meaning of *precision vs accuracy*.

**Technical relatives (used constantly in engineering)**

- **Floating-point precision:** *half precision* (float16, bfloat16) · *single precision* (float32) · *double precision* (float64)
  → *Using **half precision** halves memory traffic and doubles throughput at a small cost in **precision**.*
- **Model metrics:** *precision* = TP/(TP+FP) ≠ *accuracy* ≠ *recall*
- *precision–recall trade-off*
- *arbitrary precision* — Python `decimal` / `fractions`
- *approximate vs precise computation* → approximate computing

### 5. Native Negation & Hedge Patterns

Natives rarely cancel a verb with a negative; they restructure:
- *I would **not go so far as to say** …* (instead of ❌ *I wouldn't say that is exactly wrong*)
- *It **does not necessarily follow** that …* (instead of ❌ *It's not exactly true*)
- ***Not exactly** — but close.* (soft correction, far better than *precisely* in response to a mistake)
- ***To be precise**, … / *More precisely*, …* (self-correction upward in precision)
- ***Hardly / barely** worth mentioning* (downweight a point without denying it)
- ***To be fair**, … / *That said*, …* (concede before disagreeing)

### 6. Viet-Glish Pitfalls vs. Band 7.0+ Alternatives

| ❌ Viet-Glish / common error | ✅ Native standard | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "I want to **mention about** this problem." | "I want to **raise this issue**." / "…**mention this problem**." | `mention` is transitive — no `about` |
| "Please **mention me about** the deadline." | "Please **let me know** the deadline." | Viet-Glish; `mention` ≠ `notify` |
| "As **mention** above" | "**As mentioned** above" | Past participle required |
| "I want this **precisely**." | "I want **precisely** this." | `precisely` precedes the emphasised element |
| "It is **very precisely** defined." | "It is **precisely** defined." | `precisely` is already strong; `very` is a band-aid |
| "Please **explain precise** what you mean." | "Please **explain precisely** what you mean." | Adjective ≠ adverb |
| "In **precise**, the answer is 5." | "**To be precise**, the answer is 5." | The idiom is *to be precise* |
| "**Precisely!** Yes!" (casual chat) | "**Exactly!** You got it!" | `precisely` as a reply is stiff |
| *"I want to **raise about** the deadline."* | *"I want to **raise the deadline issue**."* | `raise` is transitive |
| "The paper **touches** the ethics topic." | "The paper **touches on** the ethics topic." | `touch on` is the phrasal verb |
| "I was **refered** to the March report." | "I was **referred** to the March report." | Past tense = `referred` |
| "A **cite site**." | "A **website**." | Not a word |
| "**Viện dẫn** nguồn A → **quote** source A" | "**Viện dẫn** nguồn A → **cite** source A; trích nguyên văn → **quote**" | *cite* ≠ *quote* |
| "**rất chính xác**" for both precise & accurate | Choose by focus: mịn → *precise*, đúng thực tế → *accurate* | Trùng nghĩa gây lỗi diễn đạt |

### 7. Cross-Domain Bridge (Tech ↔ Business ↔ Academic)

**AI / ML engineering**
- **Model cards & reports:** *The model card **states** the evaluation protocol and **cites** the benchmark datasets.* — these two verbs are the formal backbone of trustworthy documentation.
- **Terminology discipline:** *In this note, **annotation** refers to human labelling* — the *refers to* sense is how you prevent vocabulary drift inside a repository.
- **Precision claims:** in research, *precisely quantified* gains (with confidence intervals) vs vague *"significant improvement"*. Reserve *extraordinary* for praise; quantify the rest.
- **Data quality:** *state the assumption*, *state the dataset version* — reproducibility begins with explicit statements.

**Software / system design**
- **Spec language:** contracts and RFCs prefer *state* (binding) over *mention* (incidental) — a review comment that says *"please **state** the timeout explicitly"* is a design requirement, not a style note.
- **Cross-references:** *see §3.2, **refer to** the diagram below* — the *refer to* sense 1 and 3 are daily engineering register.
- **Code comments:** `// TODO:` is *"raised"* as a debt item in a postmortem; *touching on* a topic in a design doc is acceptable, but only if it is flagged.

**Business & communication**
- **Emails:** *I would like to **raise** a concern about …* (diplomatic), *to **state** clearly that …* (positioning), *just to **mention** that …* (soft downweight).
- **Meeting culture:** *raise a point* / *touch base on* — the *bring up* version is too casual for a client meeting.
- **Negotiation:** *I would **not go so far as to say** the deadline is unachievable* — the polite hedge that protects the relationship.

**Academic writing**
- Claim → evidence chain: *claim* → *cite* → *rephrase* (never *copy*). *The author **cites** three studies but **states** no method.*
- *To be precise* and *more precisely* are the two cheapest precision upgrades available in any essay.
- *It is worth mentioning that …* softens a claim; *It is worth noting that …* is the higher-band alternative.

### B. Fill the high-value phrases

### C. Rewrite the Viet-Glish
9. "I want to mention about the API latency problem." → ______
10. "Very precisely defined threshold." → ______
11. "As mention above, the contract state that payment is 50 millions." → ______

### Answer Key
1. **state** · 2. **refers** · 3. **raise** · 4. **mentioned** · 5. **touches on**
6. **precisely** · 7. **be precise** · 8. **To be precise**
9. *"I want to **raise the API latency problem**." / "…**mention the API latency problem**."*
10. *"**Precisely** defined threshold."* (drop `very`)
11. *"**As mentioned** above, the contract **states** that the payment **is** 50 million."* → better: *"…payment **is** USD 50 million."*

## 🔬 Research Journey

- **Why I'm researching this:** *đề cập* is one Vietnamese word doing four English jobs. Every technical document I write needs the right one — a *cite* where evidence is required, a *state* where a contract is implied, a *raise* where a problem enters the discussion. Choosing wrong is not a style error; it changes the legal and academic force of the sentence.
- **What I struggled with:** (a) the difference between *refer to* = "mean" and *refer to* = "point back"; (b) not knowing when *precisely* sounds like a cut rather than a compliment (Sense 4); (c) over-using *mention* as a universal translation of *đề cập*.
- **Aha moments:** (1) *state* vs *mention* is really **binding vs incidental** — that reframed how I write specs; (2) *precisely* has a rude register that dictionaries do not flag; (3) natives fix over-precise speakers with *"not exactly"* instead of negation, which is safer than anything I would have invented.
- **Career connection:** these verbs are the interface between me and the people who read my work — tech leads reviewing a PR, a bank client reading a spec, a reviewer checking a claim. Choosing *cite* instead of *claim* is the difference between an argument and evidence.
