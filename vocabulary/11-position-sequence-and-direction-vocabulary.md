## 🧠 Detailed Vocabulary Analysis

### 1. FOURTH — the ordinal, and the four idioms worth knowing

#### Ordinal system and the irregular spellings
| Number | Ordinal | Note |
|---|---|---|
| one | **first** | |
| two | **second** | |
| three | **third** | |
| four | **fourth** | `four` + `th` |
| five | **fifth** | ⚠️ `ve` → `f` |
| eight | **eighth** | `t` is silent in the base word |
| nine | **ninth** | `ne` → `n` |
| twelve | **twelfth** | `ve` → `f` |
| twenty | **twentieth** | drop the `y` |
| forty | **fortieth** | `four` → `fort` |
| ninety | **ninetieth** | drop the `y` |
| one hundred | **hundredth** | |

- Compound ordinals take **two hyphens**: *twenty-first*, *one hundred and fifth* (BrE often drops *and*).
- ⚠️ **Homophone warning: `fourth` /fɔːθ/ and `forth` /fɔːθ/ are identical.** Learn these together:
  - **forth** (formal, literary adverb) = *forward, onward* — *"back and **forth**"*, *"and so **forth**"* (≈ *v.v.*), *"from this day **forth**"* (formal, dated).
  - ❌ *"and so fourth"* is a real and embarrassing learner error.

#### Usage
- *the **fourth** of May* = **ngày 4 tháng 5** · *May **fourth*** (AmE) · *the **Fourth** of July* (Independence Day)
- *in **one fourth** of the cases* → more idiomatic: **in a quarter of the cases**
- ***three-fourths*** = ba phần tư
- *come **fourth*** (finished in that position) · *the **fourth**-largest bank* · *the **fourth** century BC*
- *the **fourth** attempt* · *the **fourth** dimension* (spacetime) · *a **fourth**-generation mobile network* / *4GL, 5GL*

#### ⚠️ The floor numbering trap (BrE vs AmE) — a real-world error
| Building | British English | American English |
|---|---|---|
| Street level | **the ground floor** | **the first floor** |
| One level up | **the first floor** | **the second floor** |
| Three levels up | **the third floor** | **the fourth floor** |

→ **The British *fourth floor* is the American *fifth floor*.** Native speakers say *"it's on the third floor"* constantly, so in any international meeting this is worth stating explicitly: *"it's three levels up, above the ground-floor lobby."*

#### The four cultural and business uses
| Expression | Vietnamese | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **break the fourth wall** | phá vỡ bức tường thứ tư | Theatre: an actor addresses the audience; film: an illusion is broken; academic writing: a speaker steps outside the frame |
| **the fourth estate** | giới báo chí | Journalism, after clergy, nobility and the commons |
| **the Fourth Industrial Revolution** | Cuộc cách mạng công nghiệp lần thứ tư | 1. steam · 2. electricity · 3. automation & computing · **4. IoT, AI, cyber-physical systems** |
| **the fourth quarter (Q4)** | quý IV | The last quarter of the financial year — a business default reading |

- The **fourth wall** metaphor is extremely productive and slightly playful: *"let me break the fourth wall for a second"* = *"here is something I would normally not say in a formal report."*
- **IR 4.0** is directly relevant to an IoT and AI student: the framing is that the fourth revolution is the first in which physical and digital systems are designed together, not merely connected.

#### ⚠️ A Vietnamese-specific trap: weekday names are ordinals in Vietnamese, but not in English
| Vietnamese | Literal | Actual weekday |
|---|---|---|
| **thứ hai** | "the second" | **Monday** |
| **thứ ba** | "the third" | **Tuesday** |
| **thứ tư** | "the fourth" | **Wednesday** |
| **thứ năm** | "the fifth" | **Thursday** |
| **thứ sáu** | "the sixth" | **Friday** |
| **thứ Bảy** | "the seventh" | **Saturday** |
| **chủ nhật** | "Lord's day" | **Sunday** |

Consequences when translating:
- *"thứ tư"* is **ambiguous** in Vietnamese: it can mean **Wednesday** *or* **the fourth**. In English, **fourth is never a weekday** — so translate by meaning, never by position: *"thứ tư tuần này"* → *"this Wednesday"*, and *"lần thứ tư"* → *"the fourth time"*.
- ⚠️ **thứ Bảy** (Saturday, capital *B*) vs **thứ bảy** (the seventh) — a genuine ambiguity in writing, which is why Vietnamese speakers say **"thứ Bảy"** with a capital for the day.
- ⚠️ English learners often produce *"I will come on the fourth of this week"* — impossible in English, and instantly recognised as a translation artefact.

### 2. STAGE — position in a process, and six other jobs

| Sense | Vietnamese | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. A step in a process** | giai đoạn, bước | *at this **stage** of the project* |
| **2. A level of development** | bậc, cấp độ, trình độ | *The technology is at an advanced **stage**.* |
| **3. Theatre** | sân khấu | *go on **stage*** · *an actor **stages** a play* |
| **4. A platform** | bậc đỡ, sân khấu, bụi giảng | *a temporary **stage** for the ceremony* |
| **5. Verb: to phase** | chia thành giai đoạn, dàn dựng | *a **staged** rollout* |
| **6. Verb: to present publicly** | trình diễn, tổ chức (sự kiện) | ***stage** a demonstration / a protest* |
| **7. Git** | đưa vào vùng chỉ | *Please **stage** the file, then commit.* |
| **8. Geology** | giai đoạn địa chất | *the sedimentary **stage*** |

#### Git sense — *stage* is a real verb
In version control, **staging** is the step between editing and committing:
```
edit → git add ("stage") → git commit → git push
```
- *"Just **stage** the two files and commit."* — this is exactly how developers talk.
- **Staged changes** = thay đổi đã `git add` nhưng chưa commit; **unstaged** = chưa `git add`.
- **staged (adj)** also means *phân tầng / theo giai đoạn*: *a **staged** migration* (migrate 5% of users first), *a **staged** deployment*.
- Full git vocabulary is in [[vocabulary/09-commit-and-working-state-terminology.md]].

#### ⚠️ `staging` has two completely different meanings
| Expression | Domain | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **the staging area** | Git | Where `git add` puts a file — vùng chờ |
| **the staging environment** | DevOps | A pre-production clone of production — môi trường thử |
| **staging data** | Data engineering | A landing area before final load (S3 staging prefix) |
| **a staging table** | Databases | A temporary table for intermediate results |

In a conversation with a DevOps engineer, *"the staging failed"* almost certainly means the **staging environment**, not `git add`. Clarify early.

#### Deployment vocabulary built on *stage*
| Phrase | Vietnamese | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **a staged / phased rollout** | triển khai theo giai đoạn | Release to a growing slice of users |
| **canary deployment** | triển khai chim hoàng yêng | A tiny slice first, with automated rollback |
| **blue–green deployment** | triển khai xanh–lục | Two identical environments, instant cutover |
| **a three-stage bootstrapper** | trình khởi tạo 3 bước | ML lifecycle: base → task-specific → production |
| **a three-stage rocket** | tên lửa 3 tầng | — |

#### Collocations
`at this stage` · `at an early / late / advanced stage` · `in its early stages` · `a two-stage process` · `the final stage` · `a key stage` · `move to the next stage` · `set the stage for` (idiomatic: *lay the groundwork for*) · `go on / off stage` · `a stage set` · `stage a protest` · `stage the changes` · `the staging area` · `stage-gate` (business) · `reach the proof-of-concept stage`

### 3. BACKWARD — direction in a sequence

**Grammar first:** the **adjective** takes no `-s`; the **adverb** usually does.
- *a **backward** glance* / *a **backward** step* (adjective)
- *he moved **backward(s)*** (adverb; **backwards** is standard in BrE, both in AmE)
- *backward **compatibility*** (adjective, before a noun) — ⚠️ but **backwards-compatible** also appears, especially in British writing

| Sense | Domain | Vietnamese | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reverse direction | General | ngược lại, lùi | *he took a **backward** step* |
| **Backward compatibility** | **Software** | **tương thích ngược** | *breaking **backward** compatibility is a red flag in a client library* |
| **Backward pass** | **Project management (CPM)** | lượt ngược | *the **backward** pass computes total float* |
| **Back-scheduling** | Project management | lên lịch ngược từ deadline | *We back-scheduled from the launch date.* |
| **Backward propagation** | **ML** | truyền ngược | ***backward** propagation reuses the forward pass's activations* |
| **Backward chaining** | **AI planning** | suy luận ngược từ đích | *a planner uses **backward** chaining from the goal* |
| **Backward induction** | Game theory / maths | lập luận ngược | ***backward** induction yields the subgame-perfect equilibrium* |
| **Backward reasoning** | Logic / psychology | suy luận ngược | *diagnosticians reason **backward** from the symptom* |
| **Lagging indicator** | **Economics** | chỉ báo **lagging** / nhìn về quá khứ | *CPI and unemployment are **backward-looking**.* |
| **Backward integration** | **Strategy** | tích hợp **ngược** (về nhà cung cấp) | ***backward** integration means owning your suppliers* |
| **Retrograde transport** | Neuroscience | vận chuyển ngược | *proteins are shipped **retrograde** along the axon* |
| **Backward masking** | Perception psychology | che khuất ngược | *a following mask hides a preceding stimulus* |

#### Forward vs backward — the pairs worth memorising

| Pair | Backward version | Forward version |
|---|---|---|
| **Reasoning** | from conclusion to premises; abduction to the best explanation | from premises to conclusion; deduction |
| **Planning** | backward chaining / means-end analysis: work back from the goal | forward chaining: apply rules to facts |
| **Scheduling** | backward pass → late start, late finish, **total float** | forward pass → early start, early finish |
| **Networks** | backward propagation: compute gradients, reuse activations | forward pass: compute activations, cache them |
| **Business** | **backward** integration → acquire suppliers | **forward** integration → acquire distribution |
| **Economics** | lagging, backward-looking indicators | leading, forward-looking indicators |
| **Integration testing** | test a module against mocks of its dependencies | test the real dependency graph |
| **Recovery** | rollback, revert, undo | roll forward, replay, compensate |

#### The engineering trade-off this exposes
**Backward compatibility vs forward design.** Every interface decision is a bet: preserve the old contract (safe, slow) or change it (clean, costly). Real systems use both — a versioned API plus a deprecation window. Note the vocabulary for the cost side: *breaking change*, *deprecation*, *migration*, *sunset*, *technical debt*.

#### Collocations
`backward-compatible` · `maintain backward compatibility` · `a backward pass` · `backward scheduling` · `backward propagation` · `backward chaining` · `backward induction` · `backward integration` · `a backward-looking indicator` · `a backward glance` · `go / move backward(s)` · `step backward` · `look backward` · `backward references` (in text) · `look backward to` · `backward-looking attitudes`

### 5. Viet-Glish Pitfalls

| ❌ Viet-Glish / common error | ✅ Native standard | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "lần thứ tư tuần này" | "**this Wednesday**" | In English, ordinals are never weekday names |
| "thứ tư họp" | "**Wednesday** meeting" / "*on Wednesday*" | Same trap |
| "I will come on the **fourth of this week**." | "I will come **on Wednesday**." | Not a possible English construction |
| "and so **fourth**" | "and so **forth**" | Homophone trap |
| "from now **fourth**" | "from now **on**" (natural) / "from this day **forth**" (formal) | *Forth*, not *fourth* |
| "Trên tầng **thứ tư**" (for a Vietnamese building) | "**the third floor**" if you mean 3 levels up in BrE usage | The floor-numbering convention differs; state it explicitly |
| "giai đoạn" for both *stage* and *phase* | Pick **stage** (a step) or **phase** (a distinct, longer part) — and be consistent | Overlapping vocabulary produces vague writing |
| "a **two stage** process" (adjective) | "a **two-stage** process" | Compound adjective needs a hyphen |
| "**staging** bị lỗi" (to a DevOps engineer) | Clarify: the **staging environment** or the **Git staging area**? | Two unrelated meanings |
| "Tôi **stage** file rồi commit" | ✅ "I **staged** the file and committed." | English past participle in a completed sequence |
| "This is **backward** thinking" (bad) | "This is **backwards** thinking" / "**regressive** thinking" | Noun needs `-s`; *regressive* is the formal adjective |
| "a **backwards** compatibility issue" | "a **backward-compatibility** issue" | Attributive adjective |
| "Nó **lùi** về phía sau" (to mean direction) | "It moved **backward(s)**." | *Lùi* also = *fall behind*; *fall behind* = tụt lại |
| "X lùi lại vì sợ" | "X **shrank back** in fear." / "X **flinched**." | `backward` is not "rụt" |
| "**3/4**" | "**three-fourths**" / "**three quarters**" / "**a quarter of**" (one in four) | Word form, not a symbol |

### 6. Cross-Domain Bridge (Tech ↔ Business ↔ Social Science)

**Computer science**
- **Backward compatibility** is a *critical property* of a public interface, and it is what makes an API a long-term liability. A client library that breaks its users is not a better library; it is a migration project they did not agree to. This connects directly to [[vocabulary/10-fragility-and-criticality-vocabulary.md]], where *critical property* is defined.
- **Backpropagation** is the reason a deep network trains: the forward pass caches activations, the backward pass applies the chain rule to reuse them. The vocabulary — *forward pass, backward pass, computational graph, gradient, reverse-mode autodiff* — is what separates a person who has coded a network from one who has only read about it.
- **Integration testing** in both directions: a *backward test* isolates a unit with mocks; a *forward test* exercises the real dependency graph.

**AI agents & planning**
- **Forward chaining** = start from known facts and fire rules; **backward chaining** = start from the goal and work out which facts would satisfy it (means-end analysis, the basis of classical planners and PDDL).
- Agent designs are usually a **hybrid**: backward chaining to decide *what to achieve*, forward chaining to decide *what to do next*. Naming the direction is what makes an agent architecture diagram legible.

**Project management**
- The **Critical Path Method** has two passes: the **forward pass** computes earliest start and finish; the **backward pass** computes latest start and finish and therefore **total float / slack**. Every delay question about a schedule is answered by the backward pass.
- **Back-scheduling** from the launch date is how teams avoid the classic failure of building right and finishing late. The calendar vocabulary for it is *milestone, critical path, slack, deadline compression (crashing), fast-tracking*.
- Links to the Agile material in `books_summaries/agile_project_management/`, where a *commitment* is compared with a *plan*.

**Economics & business**
- **Backward induction** in game theory is the method that finds the **subgame-perfect equilibrium** by reasoning from the end of a dynamic game backwards. It is also a general warning: if you only reason forwards from today's assumptions, you systematically miss what everyone else will do later.
- **Lagging versus leading indicators**: GDP, unemployment and CPI are **backward-looking**; PMI, new orders and equity prices are **forward-looking**. Policy built only on lagging data is always late — the definition of a **backward-looking policy trap**.
- **Vertical integration**: **backward** integration acquires suppliers, **forward** integration acquires distributors. The choice is a bet on bargaining power and on how much of the margin you keep.
- **Backward-bending labour supply**: at high wage levels, workers may choose more leisure than income, so the supply curve bends back. A reminder that a variable can reverse direction rather than simply grow.

**Psychology & cognitive science**
- **Backward reasoning** is how humans actually diagnose: you see the symptom and work back to the cause. **Abduction** (Peirce) is the formal name for this — inference to the best explanation. Contrast with **deduction**, which runs forward from premises.
- **Wason's selection task** showed that people evaluate *conditional* reasoning correctly in concrete "social contract" cases and poorly in abstract logic — the same person who is logically flawless about beer and prefixes fails on P→Q. Direction of reasoning is part of the finding.
- **Backward masking** in perception: a stimulus that follows a target suppresses the perception of the target. Timing, not content, determines what you see.

**Linguistics & language learning — the bridge that explains the learner**
- English is **head-initial** (*computer*, not *machine computer*); Vietnamese is **head-final** (*máy tính* — the head noun comes last). Modifier order differs accordingly.
- This single structural difference predicts most Viet-Glish habits: postposed modifiers (*"a problem I have"*), postposed relative clauses, and difficulty placing long pre-modifiers before the noun.
- **Backward substitution** is a grammar-teaching technique: you transform a sentence from its surface structure back towards an underlying form. Doing this consciously, on your own errors, is the fastest way to make the head-direction difference concrete.
- The same note explains why *"the fourth of this week"* fails: the English system has no ordinal weekday slot at all, so there is nothing to map a Vietnamese ordinal onto.

**Business & history**
- **Breaking the fourth wall** migrated from theatre to cinema, then to games and interactive fiction, and now to academic speaking (*"let me be explicit about my own assumptions"*). Recognising the move is a sign of comfortable register.
- **Q4** is a business default, which is why a year-end push is universally described as *"the fourth-quarter crunch"*.

## 🔬 Research Journey

- **Why I'm researching this:** three words I use constantly and had never examined. *Fourth* exposed a genuine translation trap that affects every Vietnamese speaker weekly, *stage* hid a git verb I had only ever met as a noun, and *backward* turned out to name four different engineering passes I already knew by heart in another language.
- **What I struggled with:** (a) I had translated *"thứ tư tuần này"* as *"the fourth of this week"* without realising English has no such construction; (b) `staging area` versus `staging environment` had never been disambiguated; (c) using *fragile* in my notes where *brittle* was meant.
- **Aha moments:** (1) Vietnamese weekday names are literal ordinals while English weekdays are not — one table explains a whole family of translation errors; (2) the CPM **backward pass** is the same word as **backpropagation**'s backward pass, and both compute what can still be absorbed because of what has already happened; (3) **backward induction** is the method that exposes what forward-only reasoning misses, which is a warning about my own project plans.
- **Career connection:** backward compatibility decides whether a client library is adoptable; the backward pass is how a schedule tells you which delay is survivable; backward chaining is how a planner decides what to do next. Three domains, one direction.
