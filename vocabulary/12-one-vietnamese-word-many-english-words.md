## 📌 Term List & Quick Reference

This note is not organised by word family. It is organised by **translation problem**: six Vietnamese words that each collapse two to sixteen different English words into one.

| Vietnamese | Default English | How many English words hide behind it |
|---|---|---|
| **cuối ngày** | **end of the day** (EOD) | 3 senses: time marker · idiom "after all" · late in the day |
| **tận dụng** | **leverage** | 9: leverage, make use of, put to use, utilize, capitalize on, harness, tap into, exploit, get the most out of |
| **giai đoạn** | **stage** | 8: stage, phase, step, milestone, period, era, epoch, leg |
| **can thiệp** | depends on object | 4 senses × 8 words: interfere, meddle, intrude, butt in, access, hack into, tamper with, snoop |
| **có sẵn** | **off-the-shelf** / **built-in** | 15: off-the-shelf, ready-made, out-of-the-box, turnkey, prebuilt, built-in, native, bundled, stock, canned, boilerplate, prefab, in stock, inherent, idiomatic |
| **ngày qua ngày** | **day after day** | 6: day after day, day by day, day in day out, days on end, every day / daily, day-to-day |

**Why this note exists:** a Vietnamese dictionary entry is a lie of compression. A native speaker who hears the English default for *giai đoạn* will assume *stage*; if you meant *phase*, the whole sentence changes meaning. The failure mode is not "ungrammatical" — it is **confidently misread**. That is why translation-by-lookup produces writing that is *almost* right, and why the errors in this diary's own LinkedIn draft were invisible to the writer.

## 🧠 Detailed Vocabulary Analysis

### 1. CUỐI NGÀY — one Vietnamese phrase, two English sentences

#### Sense A: the time marker (the dictionary answer)

| Expression | Vietnamese | Precision |
|---|---|---|
| **by the end of the day** | không muộn hơn cuối ngày | a **deadline** |
| **at the end of the day** | vào đúng thời điểm cuối ngày | a **scheduled moment** |
| **EOD** (end of day) / **COB** (close of business) | hết giờ làm việc | business deadline, **before midnight** |
| **an end-of-day report** (adjective) | báo cáo cuối ngày | a **deliverable type** |
| **late in the day** | về muộn | about **people** |

- The `by` vs `at` distinction is the whole lesson: `by EOD` = *no later than*, `at EOD` = *precisely then*.
- In business register, **EOD means end of business day**, not 24:00. For midnight use **midnight**.

#### Sense B: the idiom — the classic Viet-Glish trap

> *"[everything else is noise and] **at the end of the day** it's a capital problem."* → *"sau cùng, vấn đề chỉ là vốn"*

This sense means **"after all / when all is said and done"** and has **nothing to do with time**. Because most Vietnamese↔English glossaries map *cuối ngày* → *at the end of the day*, learners confidently produce a sentence that a native speaker reads as *"late in the evening"*.

- Safe alternatives: **ultimately** · **in the end** · **when all is said and done** · **all in all** · **net-net**.
- Related: *"**It's not the end of the world.**"*

#### Decision rule
> Is there a **clock** in the sentence? Yes → `by / at the end of the day`, EOD, COB. No → it is a conclusion, so use **ultimately / in the end**.

### 2. TẬN DỤNG — the most over-compressed verb in Vietnamese

`tận dụng` is used by Vietnamese speakers for everything from "use a library efficiently" to "exploit workers", which is why English needs nine words.

| Word | Register | Use when | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **leverage** ⭐ | tech / business / startup | the default in a CV, README or pitch | *We **leverage** PostgreSQL's server-side logic to cut round-trips.* |
| **make use of** | neutral, safe | general speech, IELTS | *I **make use of** GitHub to store my notes.* |
| **put to use** | slightly formal | turning something into actual use | ***put your skills to use*** |
| **utilize** ⚠️ | formal | see warning below | ~~*utilize the API*~~ |
| **take advantage of** | neutral ↔ negative | an opportunity, or a person | *She **took advantage of** the discount.* / ~~*took advantage of his ignorance*~~ |
| **capitalize on** | business | **extracting profit** | *Startups **capitalize on** scarcity.* (BrE: capitalise) |
| **harness** | formal, grand | **taming a large power** | ***harness** the power of AI* · *harness solar energy* |
| **tap into** | media / business | **opening a large source** | ***tap into** the open-source community* |
| **get the most out of** | conversational | maximising a person or tool | ***get the most out of** GPT-4* |
| **exploit** | technical / negative | a vulnerability, or a workforce | ***exploit** a race condition* · *firms **exploit** cheap labour* |

#### ⚠️ Do not use `utilize`
Oxford classes it as a *pedantic or formal substitute for **use***, and it has become a marker of corporate filler. IELTS Speaking examiners treat it as over-engineered. Say **use** or **make use of**.

#### ⚠️ `harness` is easy to overuse
It works because the image is literal — harnessing a horse, harnessing solar power. Used three times in one paragraph it becomes a parody of business writing (*"we harness synergies across our ecosystem"*).

#### ⚠️ Do not use `leverage` as a plain synonym for `use`
~~*I leverage my phone to call people*~~ → *"I **use** my phone to call people."* `Leverage` requires a resource with **latent advantage** being converted into output.

#### Word family
- **leverage (v)** → leveraged, leveraging
- **leverage (n)** — three other meanings: *đòn bẩy tài chính* (financial leverage); *lợi thế* (*SQL gave us real leverage*); *điểm nút* (*a policy leverage point*, after Donella Meadows — see `psychology_society/03-tragedy-of-the-commons.md`)
- **lever (n)** — a *means of influence*: *"Leverage is the ability to influence outcomes."*

#### `tận dụng tối đa` needs an extra word
`maximise` (a measurable result) · **make the most of** (an opportunity) · **get the most out of** (a person or tool) · **fully exploit the potential of** (formal) · **put to good use** (used for the *right* purpose, not for maximum) · **optimise** (made more efficient).

⚠️ Mixing registers flips the meaning: *"We leverage the model to **maximise** efficiency"* is fine; *"We leverage the model to **maximise** our profits off their data"* turns *tận dụng* into *bóc lột*.

### 3. GIAI ĐOẠN — eight English words, one Vietnamese slot

| Word | Question it answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **stage** | *which **step** is this?* | *at this **stage** of the project* |
| **phase** | *which **named part** of the project?* | *the design **phase*** |
| **step** | *which **action** in a procedure?* | ***Step 1**: install Docker* |
| **milestone** | *which **dated achievement**?* | *hit a **milestone*** |
| **period** / **stretch** | *which **measured span of time**?* | *a three-week **period*** · *the final **stretch*** |
| **era** | which **historical age**? | *the **era** of AI* |
| **epoch** | which **technical turning point**? | *the **epoch** of the Transformer* |
| **term** | which **term of office / term of study**? | *the autumn **term*** |
| **leg** | which **leg of a journey**? | *the final **leg** of the migration* |

**The rule:** if the part has a **name** → `phase`. If it is only a **position in a sequence** → `stage`.

- *"We passed the **coding stage**."* ✅
- *"We're now in the **testing phase**."* ✅
- *"We're in the **testing stage**"* ❌ — a native speaker would say *testing phase*.

#### `stage` has 8 senses of its own
Step in a process · level of development (*at an advanced stage*) · theatre (*go on stage*) · platform (*a temporary stage for the ceremony*) · **verb** to phase (*a staged rollout*) · **verb** to present publicly (*stage a protest*) · **Git** (*stage the file, then commit*) · geology.

⚠️ **`staging` is ambiguous across domains** — in Git it is where `git add` puts a file; in DevOps it is a pre-production environment; in data engineering it is a landing area. "The staging failed" to a DevOps engineer almost certainly means the *environment*. See `vocabulary/11-position-sequence-and-direction-vocabulary.md` for the full treatment.

#### `phase` traps
- **`in phase` / `out of phase`** = electrically in step / out of step — unrelated to project phases.
- **`phase out`** = to retire something gradually (*We phased out the legacy API*). **`phase in`** = to introduce gradually.
- **`phase` is an anti-pattern word in Agile.** Dividing a project into fixed phases (design → code → test) *is* Waterfall. Agile works in **sprints** and **increments** against a **roadmap**. Saying "my project has five phases" in an Agile interview triggers the Waterfall reflex. Evidence in this repo: `books_summaries/05-agile_project_management/notes/01:19`, `notes/02:136`, `notes/06:21` ("Waterfall in disguise").

#### `giai đoạn cuối cùng` is a three-way trap
Last **day** → *the last day* · final **leg** of a journey → *the final leg* · final **step** of a process → *the final stage*.

### 4. CAN THIỆP — four unrelated senses

#### Sense A: meddling in someone else's business
| Word | Force | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **interfere** | mild → negative | ***Don't interfere with** my work.* |
| **meddle** | always negative; nosy | ***Don't meddle in** my affairs.* ⚠️ connotes unwarranted curiosity |
| **intrude on / into** | violating a boundary | *The camera **intrudes on** my privacy.* |
| **butt in** | interrupting speech | *Nobody asked you — stop **butting in**.* |
| **lay hands on** | literal: touch; figurative: take control of | ***Don't lay hands on** my keyboard.* |
| **hands off** | a warning | ***Hands off** — it isn't your repo.* |

`interfere with` carries **two meanings at once**: *to obstruct* (*traffic interfered with my connection*) and *to meddle* (*stop interfering with my design decisions*). The subject decides which.

*Don't interfere with me / it* → **stay out of it** (neutral) · **mind your own business** (blunt) · **hands off** (a warning).

#### Sense B: accessing a system — the CV trap
`access` (v/n) is the neutral word: *access the database* · *you don't have access* · *an inaccessible folder* · *access rights / access control / access permission*.

⚠️ **Never translate a technical *can thiệp* with *interfere*.** "I can access the database" is correct; "I can interfere with the database" reads as sabotage.

#### Sense C: unauthorised entry or modification
| Word | Domain | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **hack into** | IT, successful | ***Hackers hacked into** the MQTT broker.* |
| **break into** | buildings, ownership | *They **broke into** the building.* |
| **tamper with** | altering something **improperly** | ***Someone tampered with** the config file.* → ***tamper-proof*** (see `git_github/01:18`) |
| **intrude** | formal intrusion | *An intruder **intruded on** the network.* |
| **snoop** | snooping on private communication | ***snooping on** MQTT traffic* |
| **unauthorised access** | fixed phrase; **HTTP 401** | relevant to PostgREST (`dev_foundation/databases/`) |

#### Sense D: the light sense — getting involved
***get involved in*** · ***jump in*** · ***have a go at** Python* · ***dip into** Python*.

#### ⚠️ `interfere` vs `intervene` — the pair most learners get backwards
| | Meaning | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| **intervene** | step in deliberately to change or fix something | 🟢 usually **good** — *UNICEF intervened to protect the children* |
| **interfere** | intrude irregularly, causing harm or noise | 🔴 usually **bad** — *static interfered with the signal* |

This is why native speakers say *the government intervened* for "chính phủ can thiệp": in English, "can thiệp" already connotes deliberate corrective action.

⚠️ **`intervent` does not exist in English.** It is a common error among natives too.

### 5. CÓ SẴN — available, and where the English genuinely branches

| Word | Precise meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **off-the-shelf** ⭐ | available from the market, usable **without customisation** | *an **off-the-shelf** solution* |
| **ready-made** | manufactured in advance | *a **ready-made** meal* |
| **ready-to-use** | usable immediately | *a **ready-to-use** API* |
| **out-of-the-box** (OOB) | works **in its delivered state** | *It works **out of the box**.* |
| **turnkey** | delivered ready to operate, handover only | *a **turnkey** solution / ship* |
| **prebuilt / precompiled** | built in advance on another machine | *a **prebuilt** Docker image* |
| **built-in** | integrated **into the device or product** | ***built-in** WiFi* |
| **native** | a version written for this platform | *a **native** Android app* |
| **bundled** | packaged together | *features **bundled with** the plan* |
| **comes as standard** | included, not an extra | *Warranty **comes as standard**.* |
| **stock** | unused, straight from stock | ***stock** phrases* · ***stock** footage* |
| **canned** | prepared in advance and **rigid** | *a **canned** answer* |
| **boilerplate** | repetitive template code or legal text | ***boilerplate** code* (see `dev_foundation/databases/README.md:27`) |
| **prefab / prefabricated** | assembled in advance | *a **prefab** house* |
| **in stock / on hand** | physically available in inventory | *Is it **in stock**?* |
| **inherent / intrinsic** | present **in the nature of the thing** | *an **inherent** risk* |
| **idiomatic** | a natural, existing expression | *an **idiomatic** way to say it* |

#### ⚠️ `có sẵn` ≠ `sẵn sàng`
| Vietnamese | Meaning | English |
|---|---|---|
| **có sẵn** | already exists, nothing more needed | **available** · **built-in** · **ready-made** |
| **sẵn sàng** | willing, prepared | **ready** · **willing** |

*"Tôi có sẵn giải pháp rồi"* → *"I already **have** a solution"* — not *"I'm ready"*.

#### ⚠️ The four biggest translation errors
| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **premium** | **in stock** / **available** / **built-in** | `premium` = **upscale, paid** — zero relation to "available". The single most common mis-rendering of *có sẵn* |
| **instant** | **ready-to-use** | `instant` = **instantaneous in time**, not pre-existing |
| **spare** | **in stock** | `spare` = **spare, backup** |
| **standby** | **in stock** / **on hand** | `standby` = **standing by, on call** |

#### Three questions that disambiguate the whole family
1. Is it **inside the product**? → **built-in**
2. Is it **on the market, un-customised**? → **off-the-shelf**
3. Is it **handed over ready to operate**? → **turnkey**

**Applied to this diary's own hardware:** the ESP32 has **built-in** WiFi and BLE, but its PSRAM is **not** built-in — it is an **external** module. Never write *"built-in PSRAM"*.

### 6. NGÀY QUA NGÀY — one idea, six words

| Phrase | Nuance | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| **day after day** | each successive day; stresses **accumulation** or **exhaustion** | neutral ↔ negative |
| **day by day** | **gradually**, little by little | usually **positive** — progress |
| **day in, day out** | relentlessly, without a break off | 🔴 **negative** — monotonous grind |
| **days on end / for days on end** | **consecutively, for many days** | stresses **duration** |
| **every day / daily** | each day, as a **schedule or habit** | neutral |
| **day-to-day** (adjective) | **day-level / short-term** | technical, operational, financial |

> *Day after day, the model's accuracy drifted further from production.*
> *Day by day, it got easier to concentrate.*
> *Day in, day out, typing reports until my wrist hurt.*
> *For days on end, the server refused connections.*
> *They handle day-to-day operations.* / *The stock showed high day-to-day volatility.*

#### ⚠️ `everyday` vs `every day`
| | Spelling | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **everyday** | one word, **adjective** | *an **everyday** problem* · ***everyday** objects* |
| **every day** | two words, **adverb** | *I study **every day**.* |

❌ *"I use this everyday."* — wrong in any register you would use professionally.

#### Other day expressions worth knowing
| Vietnamese | English |
|---|---|
| overnight | *It improved **overnight**.* · *an **overnight** success* |
| day and night / 24/7 | ngày đêm liên tục |
| one day at a time | từng ngày một (12-step idiom) |
| the daily grind 🔴 | cực nhọc hằng ngày |
| those days / back in those days | thời kỳ đó — **not** "the difficult days" |
| the early days | giai đoạn đầu |
| **the day job** | công việc ban ngày (đối lập *side project*) |
| your days are numbered | sắp hết ngày |
| any day now | sắp, bất cứ lúc nào |
| in the coming days | trong những ngày tới |

⚠️ `a day's work` (possessive, "công việc của một ngày") is a different word from `days on end`.

## 📊 Viet-Glish Pitfalls — Consolidated

| ❌ Viet-Glish | ✅ Native standard | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "cuối ngày thì vấn đề chỉ là vốn" | **ultimately** / **in the end** | Conclusion, not a time marker |
| "tận dụng điện thoại để gọi" | **use** my phone | `leverage` needs latent advantage |
| "utilize the API" | **use** the API | `utilize` is bureaucratic filler |
| "giai đoạn 2020–2025" | **the period 2020–2025** | A time span is a *period* |
| "giai đoạn cài Docker" | **Step 1: install Docker** | An action is a *step* |
| "đạt được giai đoạn quan trọng" | **hit a milestone** | An achievement is a *milestone* |
| "dự án có 5 giai đoạn" (Agile context) | **five sprints** / **increments** | Phases are the Waterfall anti-pattern |
| "tôi có thể can thiệp vào database" | **I can access the database** | Technical sense, not meddling |
| "chính phủ can thiệp để ổn định giá" | **the government intervened to** | `intervene` is the deliberate, positive sense |
| "intervent" | **interfere** / **intervene** | The word does not exist |
| "hàng có sẵn" | **in stock** / **available** | Not `premium` |
| "tôi có sẵn giải pháp" | **I already have a solution** | Not "I'm ready" |
| "I use this everyday" | **I use this every day** | Adverb takes two words |
| "ngày qua ngày" (describing a schedule) | **daily** / **every day** | Repetition ≠ schedule |
| "mọi ngày một" | **one day at a time** | Fixed idiom |

## 🔗 Cross-Domain Bridge (Language ↔ CS ↔ Business)

- **Register is a technical constraint, not decoration.** Choosing `leverage` vs `make use of` vs `utilize` changes how credible a document reads to a senior engineer in the same way choosing a data structure changes latency. A résumé that says "utilized Redis" signals a different person from one that says "leveraged Redis" — both are correct English.
- **`stage` is the one word in this note that is already a Git verb.** `git add` is called *staging*; `staged rollout` means progressive production release. The same noun generates a version-control verb, a DevOps adjective, a business process noun and a geology unit. Compare `vocabulary/09-commit-and-working-state-terminology.md`.
- **`access` is a security boundary, not a convenience.** In this repository the concrete implementations are Linux file permission triples (`dev_foundation/linux/01-file-system-paths-and-permissions.md`) and Supabase Row Level Security (`random_ideas/05-projects-review-blindsight-snakeann-joblink.md`). *Can thiệp* into data is exactly what RLS prevents.
- **`day after day` is the operating rhythm of version control.** `git log` is a scannable record of a *day after day* discipline; `git diff` is the review mechanism that turns a skipped day into a visible, recoverable state. This is why the same author uses both habits together.
- **`have ready money` / `have a day off`** are exceptions where `ready` means *readily available* — memorise them as fixed phrases rather than generalising the pattern.

## 📝 Research Journey

- **Why:** Six Vietnamese words were translated in one session, and every one of them turned out to hide between 3 and 15 English choices. The interesting object is not the word — it is the **compression asymmetry** between the two languages. Vietnamese can afford vagueness; English pays for it with misreading.
- **Struggle:** The hardest habit was accepting that a *dictionary answer* is only the first of several correct answers, and that **register** (not accuracy) is what actually decides whether a sentence sounds professional. The second hardest was resisting the "one Vietnamese word = one English word" mental reflex when writing.
- **Aha moment:** The most useful discovery was that **`by` vs `at`** and **`have` vs `be ready`** are the two smallest edits in this note and the two biggest changes in meaning. Precise English is mostly a matter of prepositions and auxiliaries, not vocabulary size.
- **Career link:** As an AI Engineer, precise English is a productivity tool, not a soft skill. Reading a paper, filing an issue, or explaining a design decision all depend on choosing the exact word — and on knowing which of six near-synonyms the reader will interpret literally. This note is also the vocabulary spine for public technical writing (LinkedIn, README, design docs), where a mis-chosen register costs credibility directly.
