The passage describes the **global apparel market**: producers compete on **prestige** versus **affordable** price, consumers grow increasingly **concerned** about **sustainability** and become **conscious** buyers, brands must find a **competitive edge**, and the whole industry spans continents with a **massive** footprint. **Why this note exists:** these words look ordinary, but three of them are **false friends in disguise** for a Vietnamese learner. **Durable** is not "lasting for a fixed time" (that is *long-lasting* vs *durable* — a subtle register split). **Appeal** is a noun *and* a verb with two unrelated verb senses (*attract* vs *formally request*). **Concern / concerned** fork into "worry" and "be about/involved in" — and choosing the wrong fork makes a sentence nonsensical. And **sustainable vs. conscious** are the two words every ESG conversation now leans on, so getting their collocations right is professional, not academic.

## 🧠 Detailed Vocabulary Analysis

### 1. GARMENTS — `/ˈɡɑːrmənts/`

#### Definition & domain context
- **Noun (countable):** a single item of clothing. Formal/industry voice for *clothes*.
- The register ladder matters because IELTS Task 1/2 and business writing reward the right one:

| Word | Register | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **clothes** | everyday | *I need new clothes.* |
| **clothing** | neutral, uncountable | *the clothing industry* |
| **garments** | formal / trade | *ready-made garments* |
| **apparel** | AmE, business | *the apparel sector* |
| **attire** | very formal / ceremonial | *business attire* |

- Note: **clothing** and **apparel** are uncountable; **garments** is countable (*a garment*, *three garments*). *Attire* is also uncountable.

#### Collocations & native pairings
- **ready-made garments (RMG)** — the industry term, especially in Bangladesh/Vietnam export discourse, and the exact phrase a *The Guardian* or *Reuters* piece would use
- **garment {industry, factory, worker, sector, exports}** · **a protective / outer / under garment**
- **garment district** — a neighbourhood of clothing businesses (New York's, Los Angeles's)

#### Viet-Glish pitfall vs. Band 7.0+ alternative
- ❌ *Viet-Glish mistake:* "The garments industry is important for Vietnam."
- ✅ *Native standard:* "The **garment** industry is vital to Vietnam's exports" — in the compound, *garment* stays **singular** and functions as a noun modifier. (*Garments industry* is a common slip; *garment industry* is standard.)

### 2. FABRICS — `/ˈfæbrɪks/`

#### Definition & domain context
- **Noun:** a material produced by weaving, knitting, or bonding fibres — the *made* cloth. **Fabric** = the material; **textile** = the broader term covering fibres, yarns *and* fabric, and used in industry/statistics.
- ⚠ **Second sense:** *fabric* also means **the structure of a society/building** — *the social fabric*, *the fabric of the building*. This figurative sense is frequent in essays and is a common IELTS Reading meaning.

| Term | Scope |
|---|---|
| **fibre / fiber** | the raw strand (cotton, polyester, wool) |
| **yarn** | spun fibres |
| **fabric** | woven/knitted cloth |
| **textile** | overarching term: fibre + yarn + fabric; also *textile industry* |
| **cloth** | a piece of fabric (uncountable, everyday) |

#### Collocations & native pairings
- **natural / synthetic / blended fabrics** · **a woven / knitted fabric** · **breathable / waterproof / durable fabric** · **the fabric of {society, the building, the city}**
- **cotton / silk / linen / wool / polyester fabric**

#### Viet-Glish pitfall vs. Band 7.0+ alternative
- ❌ *Viet-Glish mistake:* "This shirt uses a good textile."
- ✅ *Native standard:* "This shirt is made from a good **fabric**" / "high-quality **textile**" — a single shirt is *fabric*; *textile* is the category/industry word.

### 3. EMBROIDERY — `/ɪmˈbrɔɪdəri/`

#### Definition & domain context
- **Noun:** the craft and the finished work of decorating fabric with needle-and-thread stitching; also **the patterns** created that way. Uncountable when the craft or product as a whole (*the embroidery on the dress*), but the countable **embroideries** exists for distinct pieces works.
- Verb: **embroider**; a person: **embroiderer**. Figuratively, **to embroider** means to **add decorative/exaggerated detail to a story** — *a heavily embroidered account* (link to note 17's *exaggeration*).

#### Collocations & native pairings
- **hand / machine embroidery** · **gold-thread / silk embroidery** · **a panel of embroidery** · **beautifully embroidered**
- Cultural hook: embroidery is a flagship **traditional craft** for many developing economies — Vietnamese *thêu*, and the *áo dài* tradition — and it sits at the **heritage ↔ mass-production** tension the passage is about.

### 4. DURABLE — `/ˈdʊrəbl/`

#### Definition & domain context
- **Adjective:** able to withstand wear, pressure, or damage; long-lasting. The marketing/engineering word for "it won't fall apart."
- **The important register split — durable vs. lasting vs. sustainable:**

| Word | Means | Typical subject |
|---|---|---|
| **durable** | resists wear/damage | goods, materials, fabric, equipment |
| **lasting** | continues for a long time | impression, peace, effect |
| **long-lasting** | (everyday) continues a long time | flavour, battery, makeup |
| **sustainable** | can continue without depletion | systems, practices, growth |
| **resilient** | recovers from shocks | economies, people, systems |

- **Durability** (n) = the property; in engineering it is a measurable spec (*wear cycles, tensile strength*). This is the **sustainability overlap**: a **durable** garment is worn longer, which is *one* pillar of **sustainable** fashion — but they are not synonyms. Durable is a *product property*; sustainable is a *system property*.

#### Collocations & native pairings
- **durable {goods, fabric, material, design, construction}** · **highly / extremely / reasonably durable** · **durability test / durability and reliability**
- Economics: **consumer durable** = a long-lived good (car, fridge, furniture) — the same adjective as a noun-of-category. (Contrast a **fast-moving consumer good / FMCG**.)

#### Viet-Glish pitfall vs. Band 7.0+ alternative
- ❌ *Viet-Glish mistake:* "This bag is very durable for many years."
- ✅ *Native standard:* "This bag is **built to last** / highly **durable** — it should **last for years.**" (Durable is a quality; *last* + duration carries the time.)

### 5. PRESTIGE — `/preˈstiːʒ/`

#### Definition & domain context
- **Noun (uncountable):** the respect, admiration, and status a person, brand, or institution commands — usually earned, usually scarce. Latin *praestigium* → "illusion, trick" → modern "standing". Note the stress on the **second** syllable and the **soft French g** (`/ʒ/`).
- **Adjective form:** **prestigious** — the one to *use*; *prestige* as a modifier (*prestige brand*) is awkward.

#### Collocations & native pairings
- **a prestigious {university, award, journal, brand, address}** · **gain / enjoy / confer prestige** · **prestige pricing** (a strategy: price *high* deliberately, because price itself signals quality — the opposite of *affordable*)
- **a matter of prestige** · **status symbol** (the consumer-behaviour term for an object bought mainly for prestige)

#### Viet-Glish pitfall vs. Band 7.0+ alternative
- ❌ *Viet-Glish mistake:* "This is a prestige brand, so it is expensive."
- ✅ *Native standard:* "This is a **prestigious** brand, which justifies its **premium price.**"

### 6. AFFORDABLE — `/əˈfɔːrdəbl/`

#### Definition & domain context
- **Adjective:** cheap enough that a given person/group can pay for it without difficulty. It is **relative** — affordable *for whom?* — and in housing economics it has a technical definition (spending no more than ~30% of income on rent/mortgage). This is why **"affordable" is a policy word, not a synonym for "cheap."**
- Verb: **afford** (can afford to + verb; can afford + noun). Note: *afford* is almost always used with **can / could / be able to** — *"I afford a house"* is wrong.

#### Register ladder for price
- **cheap / budget** (low, can sound negative) → **affordable** (low enough, positive/policy) → **reasonably priced** (neutral, positive) → **mid-range** → **premium** → **luxury / high-end** (top). **Inexpensive** is the polite euphemism for *cheap*.

#### Collocations & native pairings
- **affordable {housing, healthcare, price, option}** · **make sth affordable (for sb)** · **affordability** (n)
- ⚠ **"affordable luxury"** — a marketing oxymoron used to sell *accessible prestige*; note how it deliberately pairs terms 5 and 6.

#### Viet-Glish pitfall vs. Band 7.0+ alternative
- ❌ *Viet-Glish mistake:* "Students cannot afford for the expensive clothes."
- ✅ *Native standard:* "Students **cannot afford** expensive clothes" (no *for*, no *the* in the generic sense).

### 7. APPEAL — `/əˈpiːl/`

#### Definition & domain context
- **Noun:** attractiveness, the quality of being liked — *consumer appeal*, *mass appeal*, *sex appeal*, *curb appeal* (housing).
- **Verb (two distinct senses — the trap!):**
  1. **to attract / interest** — *the design appeals to younger buyers* (note the preposition **to**)
  2. **to make a formal, serious request** — *the company appealed against the ruling*, *the charity appealed for donations* (preposition **for** / **against**)
- **Adjective:** **appealing** (attractive) — also **appetising**, **alluring**, **compelling**, **enticing**.

#### Collocations & native pairings
- **have (a) wide / broad / mass / popular / limited appeal** · **lose / boost its appeal** · **appeal to {young people, the mass market, a niche}**
- **appeal for {help, funds, calm}** · **appeal against a decision / a sentence**
- Marketing frame: **appeal** (why people want it) vs **reach** (how many see it) vs **conversion** (how many buy) — the three metrics of a campaign.

#### Viet-Glish pitfall vs. Band 7.0+ alternative
- ❌ *Viet-Glish mistake:* "This style appeals many teenagers."
- ✅ *Native standard:* "This style **appeals to** many teenagers" (the verb *appeal* to attract **always** takes *to*).

### 8. EDGE — `/edʒ/`

#### Definition & domain context
- **Noun (literal):** the sharp side of a blade; the boundary/rim of a surface.
- **Noun (business idiom — the passage's use):** **a competitive advantage** — *a competitive edge*, *a decisive edge*, *a slight edge*.
- The business phrase **"competitive edge"** is essentially fixed; its near-synonyms are **competitive advantage** (formal, more specific in economics), **upper hand** (idiom), **leg up** (informal), **differentiator**.

#### Collocations, idioms & native pairings
- **a competitive / decisive / slight / technological edge** · **gain / lose / maintain / sharpen an edge** · **have an edge over sb**
- **on edge** (nervous, tense) · **cutting edge** (adj: the most advanced — *cutting-edge technology*) · **take the edge off** (to reduce the intensity of something unpleasant) · **edge sb out** (to beat narrowly)
- Note the adjective **edgy**: (1) nervous; (2) fashionably bold/risky — a second heteronym-of-sense to watch.

#### Viet-Glish pitfall vs. Band 7.0+ alternative
- ❌ *Viet-Glish mistake:* "We need more edge than our competitor."
- ✅ *Native standard:* "We need a **competitive edge over** our rivals."

### 9. CONTINENT — `/ˈkɑːntɪnənt/`

#### Definition & domain context
- **Noun:** one of Earth's seven large continuous landmasses (Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Europe, North America, Australia/Oceania, South America). Also **the Continent** = mainland Europe (BrE).
- **Adjective:** **continental** — *a continental breakfast*, *continental climate*, *continental drift*.

#### Collocations & native pairings
- **across / on every / throughout the continent** · **the African / Asian / European continent**
- **continental {climate, drift, shelf, Europe, breakfast}** · **cross-continental** (adj — spanning continents, as in *a cross-continental supply chain*)
- ⚠ The supply-chain frame: **offshoring** (moving production to another country) vs **nearshoring** (to a nearby country) vs **reshoring** (back home) — the geography of the garment industry, which is *global* and **spans continents**.

### 10. MASSIVE — `/ˈmæsɪv/`

#### Definition & domain context
- **Adjective:** very large, heavy, and solid. The everyday intensifier for magnitude.
- **The register ladder for "big"** (IELTS Writing loves this):

| Word | Intensity / nuance |
|---|---|
| **large / big** | neutral base |
| **substantial / considerable** | formal, measured |
| **significant** | formal, "important in effect" |
| **enormous / immense / vast** | very large |
| **massive** | very large *and* heavy/impactful |
| **colossal / gigantic / mammoth** | emphatic, emotive |

- Note the **scale vs. number distinction**: *massive* suits a *quantity, impact, or physical bulk* (*massive investment, massive scale, massive carbon footprint*), while **numerous** is for countable *number* (*numerous factories*, not *massive factories* if you mean "many").

#### Collocations & native pairings
- **massive {scale, impact, investment, growth, footprint, amount}** · **on a massive scale** · **a massive increase / reduction**
- Near-synonym pairing for essays: **"a massive (not numerous) amount of waste"** vs **"numerous (not massive) factories"**.

#### Viet-Glish pitfall vs. Band 7.0+ alternative
- ❌ *Viet-Glish mistake:* "There are massive factories in the city."
- ✅ *Native standard:* "There are **numerous** factories" (count) OR "The factory has a **massive footprint**" (scale).

### 11. ORBIT — `/ˈɔːrbɪt/`

#### Definition & domain context
- **Noun:** the curved, usually elliptical path of one body around another under gravity — a satellite *in orbit*; the Moon *in orbit around* Earth.
- **Verb:** to move in such a path — *the satellite orbits the Earth*. Note: **orbit** takes a direct object (*orbit the Earth*), unlike *circle around*.
- **Figurative sense (the passage's likely use):** **sphere of influence** — *within the orbit of*, *the orbit of the fashion capitals* = the circle of cities/people that dominate the industry. This "social orbit" sense is common in quality journalism and essays.

#### Collocations & native pairings
- **in orbit** · **go into / launch into orbit** · **a low-Earth / geostationary orbit (LEO / GEO)** · **orbital {period, mechanics, debris}**
- **bring sth within one's orbit** · **enter / leave sb's orbit** · **the orbit of {influence, power, fashion capitals}**
- Physics hook: Kepler's laws govern the ellipse; gravity provides the centripetal force $F = \frac{GMm}{r^2}$; the orbital period follows $T^2 \propto a^3$ (semi-major axis). Not needed for IELTS, but it makes the word memorable.

#### Viet-Glish pitfall vs. Band 7.0+ alternative
- ❌ *Viet-Glish mistake:* "The satellite orbits around the Earth."
- ✅ *Native standard:* "The satellite **orbits the Earth**" (no *around* with the transitive verb — though *moves around the Earth* is fine).

### 12. SUSTAINABLE — `/səˈsteɪnəbl/`

#### Definition & domain context
- **Adjective:** able to be maintained over the long term without exhausting resources or causing unacceptable harm. From *sustain* (to keep going) + *-able*.
- **The settled definition** (Brundtland Report, 1987): development that "meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs."
- **The three pillars (the "triple bottom line") — very useful for IELTS and business writing:**

| Pillar | Question it asks | Fashion example |
|---|---|---|
| **Environmental** | Does it respect planetary limits? | organic cotton, low-water dyeing, circularity |
| **Social** | Does it treat people fairly? | fair wages, no child labour, safe factories |
| **Economic** | Can the business continue to operate? | viable margins, long-term demand |

⚠ **Sustainability ≠ eco-friendly.** *Eco-friendly* is a narrower, more marketing-driven claim (low environmental harm); *sustainable* is the systems word covering all three pillars. And beware **greenwashing** — a sustainability *claim* without evidence.

#### Word family & collocations
- **sustain** (v) · **sustainable** (adj) · **sustainability** (n) · **unsustainable** (adj) · **sustainably** (adv)
- **sustainable {development, fashion, growth, agriculture, sourcing, business model}** · **environmentally / socially / economically sustainable**
- **the sustainability agenda / goals** · **ESG** (Environmental, Social, Governance) — the reporting frame companies now use.

#### Viet-Glish pitfall vs. Band 7.0+ alternative
- ❌ *Viet-Glish mistake:* "We need to sustainable the environment."
- ✅ *Native standard:* "We need to make the industry **more sustainable**" / "We need to **sustain** the environment" (*sustainable* is the adjective; do not verb it).

### 13. CONSCIOUS — `/ˈkɑːnʃəs/`

#### Definition & domain context
- **Adjective:** (1) **awake and aware** — *the patient was conscious*; (2) **aware of something** — *conscious of the risk*; (3) **deliberate, intentional** — *a conscious decision*; (4) **socially/environmentally aware** — *a conscious consumer* (the passage's sense).
- ⚠ **Conscious vs. conscientious** — a crucial IELTS pair:
  - **conscious** = *aware* (of something), or *deliberate*
  - **conscientious** = *careful, diligent, dutiful* (a personality trait — see note 13's Big Five mapping, where *conscientiousness* is one of the OCEAN traits)

#### Word family & collocations
- **conscious** (adj) · **consciously** (adv) · **consciousness** (n) · **subconscious / unconscious** (adj) · **self-conscious** (adj — shy, embarrassed about oneself)
- **environmentally / socially / ethically conscious consumers** · **a conscious effort / decision / choice** · **become conscious of**

#### Viet-Glish pitfall vs. Band 7.0+ alternative
- ❌ *Viet-Glish mistake:* "He is very conscious, so he always finishes his work."
- ✅ *Native standard:* "He is very **conscientious**, so he always finishes his work" (that is diligence, not awareness — *conscious* would be wrong here).

### 14-15. CONCERN — `/kənˈsɜːrn/` · CONCERNED — `/kənˈsɜːrnd/`

#### Definition & domain context
- **Concern (noun):** (1) **worry** — *concerns about safety*; (2) **a matter of interest/responsibility** — *that's not my concern*; (3) **a company/firm** — *a going concern* (business English).
- **Concern (verb):** (1) **to worry** — *the trend concerns us*; (2) **to be about / relate to** — *the report concerns labour practices*; (3) **to involve** — *this concerns everyone*.
- **Concerned (adj):** (1) **worried** — *concerned consumers*; (2) **involved / affected** — *all parties concerned*. The **placement** changes the sense: *the people concerned* (the ones affected) vs *a concerned person* (a worried one).

⚠ **The ambiguity test:** *"The authorities are concerned"* = worried. *"The authorities concerned"* = the relevant ones. Word order is meaning here.

#### Collocations & native pairings
- **express / raise / voice concern(s) about** · **growing / widespread / mounting concern** · **a cause for concern** · **concern for {safety, the environment}**
- **be concerned about / with / that …** · **as far as … is concerned** · **all parties concerned**
- **Concern** is also the plain register of **anxiety** in formal writing: *anxiety* (clinical/strong), *worry* (everyday), *concern* (measured, professional).

#### Viet-Glish pitfall vs. Band 7.0+ alternative
- ❌ *Viet-Glish mistake:* "Consumers are concerning about the environment."
- ✅ *Native standard:* "Consumers **are concerned about** the environment" / "the environment **is a growing concern for** consumers." (*Concerning* as an adjective means "worrying," as in *a concerning trend* — also correct, but a different speaker.)

## 🔗 Cross-Domain Bridge (Tech ↔ Business / Economics)

1. **"Affordable" is a price-positioning strategy, not just a low price.**
   The apparel market is a classic **vertical differentiation** game: **prestige pricing** at the top (price signals quality), **affordable** in the middle (price signals access), **budget** at the bottom (price signals necessity). Firms choose a position because moving *down* is easy and moving *up* is almost impossible (the "asymmetric brand ladder") — which is exactly why *"affordable luxury"* exists as a marketing category. The same logic governs SaaS tiers (free / pro / enterprise) and cloud pricing.
2. **Sustainability is an externality problem with a supply-chain solution.**
   A garment's true cost includes water use, $\mathrm{CO_2}$, and labour conditions that the sticker price usually omits — the **negative externalities** from note 17 §Bridge. The industry's answer is **traceability** and **circularity**: blockchain or QR-based **supply-chain provenance**, resale/repair platforms, and **ESG reporting**. This is a data problem as much as an ethics problem — you cannot report what you cannot trace, which is why *sustainability* and *monitoring* (note 17) are the same technical loop applied to the supply chain.
3. **"Conscious consumer" is a measurable behaviour, not a slogan.**
   Behavioural economics separates **stated preference** (what people say they care about) from **revealed preference** (what they actually buy) — and the gap is large: surveys show high willingness to pay for sustainable goods, but basket data shows price and style still dominate. Sustainable fashion succeeds precisely when it *aligns* with prestige and durability (a good bought once and worn long) rather than asking consumers to sacrifice. That is the difference between **marketing claim** and **incentive-compatible design**.

- **Why I'm researching this:** the batch arrived as a single IELTS/business vocabulary request about the apparel industry. It is squarely in the **Economics/Business** zone of the knowledge base — the interplay of **prestige** and **affordable** pricing, **competitive edge**, and the fast-growing **sustainability/ESG** agenda. As an AI Engineer aiming at AI applied to economics/business, this is the vocabulary of **market positioning** and **sustainability reporting** — both of which increasingly sit on top of data systems.
- **What I struggled with:** the **false friends**. *Durable* vs *lasting* (a quality vs a duration); *appeal* as *attract* vs *formally request* (different prepositions); *concern* as *worry* vs *be about*; *conscious* vs *conscientious* (awareness vs diligence — and note 13's Big Five makes *conscientiousness* a technical term). The fix was to test every word in the *Vietnamese sentence I actually wanted to say* and see whether the English word preserved the sense.
- **Aha moments:** (1) **"Affordable" is a positioning word, not a synonym for "cheap"** — and *"affordable luxury"* is a deliberate oxymoron pairing terms 5 and 6. (2) **Sustainability is a *systems* word with three pillars**, where *eco-friendly* is only the environmental one — and *greenwashing* is the failure mode. (3) **"Competitive edge" is basically fixed**; the productive variation is the verb (*gain, sharpen, maintain*), not the noun. (4) **Sustainable fashion works when it aligns with prestige and durability** rather than demanding sacrifice — an incentive-compatibility insight straight from behavioural economics.
- **Career link:** an AI Engineer building systems for retail, fashion, or ESG analytics needs exactly this vocabulary — *demand forecasting*, *price positioning*, *traceability*, *sustainability metrics*. Being able to write a clear product or research note that separates **stated** from **revealed** preference, and **marketing claim** from **verifiable metric**, is what makes an AI solution credible in a business context rather than merely technical.
