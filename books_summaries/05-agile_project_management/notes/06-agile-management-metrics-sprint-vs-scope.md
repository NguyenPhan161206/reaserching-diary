## ❓ Question 1: What is Velocity, and why do we use Story Points instead of hours for estimation?
### 📚 Detailed answer
- **Explanation:** Velocity is the total number of Story Points completed by a team in a single Sprint. Story Points measure relative complexity and uncertainty, avoiding the "optimism bias" inherent in guessing hours.
- **Example / Formula:** If a team completes 10, 13, and 12 points over three sprints, their average velocity is ~11.5 points/sprint. If the backlog has 50 points remaining, forecasting completion takes ~4-5 sprints.
- **Why it matters:** It provides reliable statistical forecasting based on historical performance rather than guesswork, enabling better predictability without micromanagement.

## ❓ Question 2: What is the core difference between Sprint and Scope, and how does the Inverted Iron Triangle work?
### 📚 Detailed answer
- **Explanation:** 
  - **Sprint (Timebox):** A fixed, unchangeable period (e.g., exactly 2 weeks). The train must depart on time; deadlines are never extended.
  - **Scope (Features/Work):** The variable set of features or tasks assigned to that Sprint. Scope can and should be flexed or trimmed when roadblocks arise.
  - **Inverted Iron Triangle:** Traditional project management fixes scope and flexes time/cost. Agile fixes time and cost while flexing scope to protect quality and core value (MVP).
- **Example / Crisis Handling (TBS Project):** In the Truck Blind Sight project, if hardware sensor noise troubleshooting takes longer than expected and threatens the cloud integration task, an Agile PM does *not* delay the sprint deadline. Instead, they demo the completed hardware noise fix and negotiate moving the cloud task to the next sprint.
- **Why it matters:** It prevents "Waterfall in disguise" and teaches teams to manage risk by shedding non-essential scope rather than breaking time commitments.
