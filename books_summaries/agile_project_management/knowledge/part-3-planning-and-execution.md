# Part 3: Agile Planning and Execution

## 🧠 Detailed Analysis

### Chapter 9: Defining Product Vision and Roadmap
- **Progressive Elaboration:** Agile doesn't abandon planning; it plans continuously. Detail is added "just-in-time" as more is learned.
- **The Vision:** The overarching goal that answers the "Why". It anchors the entire project.
- **The Roadmap:** A high-level, visual summary of the product's direction over time (often arranged by themes or epics). It is flexible, not a fixed Gantt chart.

### Chapter 10: Planning Releases and Sprints
- **User Stories:** Requirements written strictly from the user's perspective. 
  - *Format:* "As a [type of user], I want [some goal] so that [some reason]."
- **Estimation:** Agile uses relative estimation (e.g., Story Points using the Fibonacci sequence) instead of absolute time estimates in hours. Techniques include *Planning Poker* and *Affinity Estimating*.
- **Release vs. Sprint:** 
  - *Release Planning* looks at a multi-sprint horizon to deploy a larger chunk of business value. 
  - *Sprint Planning* is tactical; it selects the exact User Stories the team commits to finishing in the upcoming iteration (usually 1-4 weeks).

### Chapter 11: Working throughout the Day
- **The Daily Scrum:** A strict 15-minute timeboxed daily coordination meeting for the Development Team. It is *not* a status report to management, but a micro-planning session for the next 24 hours.
  - *Three standard questions:* What did I do yesterday? What will I do today? Are there any impediments?
- **Information Radiators:** Highly visible charts (like Kanban task boards or Sprint Burndown charts) that automatically communicate team progress without anyone needing to write a report.
- **Done Means Done:** Work must meet the explicit "Definition of Done" (shippable functionality, fully tested, documented) to avoid accumulating technical debt.

### Chapter 12: Showcasing Work, Inspecting, and Adapting
- **The Sprint Review (Showcase):** Demonstrating the working software to stakeholders at the end of the Sprint to gather feedback. The focus is entirely on the product.
- **The Sprint Retrospective:** Arguably the most crucial meeting for continuous improvement. The team reflects on *how* they worked during the sprint and identifies actionable process improvements to implement in the very next sprint.
