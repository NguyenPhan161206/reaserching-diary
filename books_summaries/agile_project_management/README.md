## 📌 Key Takeaways (TL;DR)
- Agile is a mindset (being Agile), not just a process (doing Agile). It focuses on continuous delivery of value.
- Projects are about dates and budgets; Products are about solving user problems. Agile shifts focus to Product Management.
- Success requires cross-functional, self-organizing, and dedicated teams.
- "Failing fast" is a feature, not a bug, allowing teams to pivot before spending massive resources.

## 📖 Book Overview
- **Author background:** Mark C. Layton (and others), experts in Agile transformation.
- **Publication year:** Various editions (For Dummies series).
- **Core thesis:** Traditional project management is too rigid for the modern knowledge economy. Agility allows teams to navigate uncertainty, reduce waste, and deliver products that customers actually want.
- **Target audience:** IT professionals, Project Managers, and Developers (including AI Engineers) looking to transition to Agile or fix broken Agile implementations.

## 📝 Research Journey
- **Why I read this:** As an AI Engineer, building a technically sound model is useless if it doesn't solve the user's problem. Agile helps align tech development with business value.
- **Biggest surprise:** Agile management is completely different from traditional management. The manager becomes a "servant-leader" rather than a taskmaster.
- **Changed my mind about:** Planning. Agile doesn't mean "no planning." It means continuous, adaptive planning at multiple levels (Vision -> Roadmap -> Release -> Sprint).
- **Will implement:** Using timeboxed Sprints for my AI research to force myself to deliver a baseline model quickly instead of getting stuck in endless optimization.
